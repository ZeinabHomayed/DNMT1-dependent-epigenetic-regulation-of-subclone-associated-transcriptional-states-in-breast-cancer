# DNMT1-dependent-epigenetic-regulation-of-subclone-associated-transcriptional-states-in-breast-cancer

Intratumoral heterogeneity (ITH) is a major determinant of therapeutic resistance, yet the
respective contributions of genetic and epigenetic regulation to transcriptional diversity remain
incompletely understood, and the extent to which subclonal genetic variations functionally
constrain tumor cell states is still debated. By integrating single-cell RNA sequencing data from
more than one hundred primary breast tumors with computational inference of copy number
alterations, we show that subclonal copy number alterations significantly contribute to
transcriptional heterogeneity in a substantial fraction of breast tumors. However, their impact is
not unrestricted: genetically distinct subclones converge toward a limited set of recurrent
transcriptional programs that define stable, functionally distinct cellular states with differential
therapeutic sensitivities, as validated using breast cancer cell lines, patient-derived xenografts,
and organoid models. Mechanistically, we identify DNA methyltransferase 1 (DNMT1) as a key
epigenetic factor stabilizing these states and constraining spontaneous transitions between them.
Pharmacological inhibition of DNMT1 reshapes the transcriptional landscape, enabling transitions
between otherwise stable states, partially overriding genetic constraints in a subset of models,
and promoting treatment-sensitive cellular states. Our findings support a model in which genetic
alterations define a constrained transcriptional state space dynamically regulated by DNMT1,
identifying tumor plasticity as a targetable process.
